The layer, until now called Urban Accessibility P.O.V., puts the Atlas’s two dimensions side by side. **Proximity** is what is close by on foot, the dimension of the 15-minute city. **Opportunity** is what public transport connects you to across the city, the dimension of CityChrone. Both are scored on the same cells by the P.O.V. framework: how many services a person reaches by walking in a short time, and by public transport in a longer one.

## Four zones, new names

Split at the city’s medians, every cell falls in one of four zones. The names now say what a place has rather than what it lacks.

![The four zones: Close & Connected, Close, Connected and Total isolation](close-and-connected/quadrants.webp "The medians are weighted by residents, not taken over cells: in Rome, proximity 2,591 and opportunity 9,435. The paper’s names were inclusion, spatial isolation, social isolation and total isolation.")

The four are not a ranking with Close & Connected on top. A place that is Close needs transit; a place that is Connected needs shops, schools and services where today there is only a station. One score for both would prescribe the same cure to each.

## New view: Both scores at once

Zones draw hard edges: a cell just above the median and one far above it get the same colour. The **Both** view drops the edges. Each score is ranked among the city’s residents, and the two ranks mix into one colour: light blue where a place has more close by than most of the city, pink where transit connects it to more, deep indigo where it has both, pale where it has little of either.

![New York, zones and Both](close-and-connected/zones-both.webp "The dashed lines across the square are the city’s medians, where the zones split it. Like the zones, the colours compare places within a city, not cities.") {wide}

## New view: The plot

The map shows where; the plot shows how far. It places every inhabited cell by its two scores instead of its location: opportunity along the bottom, proximity up the side, each dot sized by its residents and coloured by its zone. The dashed lines are the city’s medians, so each quarter of the plot is one zone. Here are Rome’s 7,043 inhabited cells.

![Rome, every inhabited cell by its two scores](close-and-connected/scatter.webp "On the site the plot is a view of the city map, on a computer: hovering a dot reads it, clicking selects its cell on the map.")

## Cells and residents tell different stories

In Rome 83.0% of cells are in total isolation, but only 46.1% of residents live in them: isolated cells are large and thinly settled. In New York the figures are 54.1% of cells and 41.0% of residents, in Paris 50.3% and 42.5%. The site always publishes both and says which one it shows.
